# Phase 4: Social verbs

**Goal:** can two or more players actually DO things together?

| Verb | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Invite** | Tap a player, Invite, choose a place (or "Come to me"). They get a card for 20 seconds. If they accept they walk there, across floors if needed, and you are told they are coming |
| **Join** | "Join them" does the natural thing: sit beside them if they are sitting or eating, follow them if they are walking, otherwise stand next to them and face them |
| **Follow** | Walk with someone, keeping a personal gap so a crowd spreads out. Stops if you lose them (about 28 tiles) or press Stop. Up to 6 followers each, and no loops |
| **Give** | Money in 10, 50 or 100 LD amounts, or your plate. Needs you within 3 tiles |
| **Help** | "Ask for help" shows a hand over your head. Someone nearby taps you, Help them. Stay within about 4 tiles for 7 seconds. The helper earns 15 LD, but not repeatedly from the same person (5 minute cooldown per pair) |
| **Mischief** | Take someone's food (they see "chase them", and a Take plate back button), or a harmless prank |

## Food
Buy a plate for 10 LD at the Back Palaver stall. Sit down and you eat it for 8 seconds, with a small animation. Eating is social: people can sit and join you.

## Mischief boundaries (the "stories, not resentment" rule)
- Opt-out for each player, both directions (if either side is off, nothing happens).
- Only food you are holding is takeable, never money, and the victim can take it back.
- A stolen plate is "hot" for 60 seconds and the victim gets a 10 second shield, a 90 second cooldown before they can be robbed again, and the thief a 20 second cooldown.
- Pranks: 15 seconds per pair, 4 per minute, always harmless (a laugh and a shake).
- Everyone nearby sees the reaction so the moment becomes a scene.

## Quiet logging
The world calls a hook for each meaningful event (followed, joined, ate_together, helped, gave_money, gave_food, stole, reclaimed, pranked, invite_accepted). Nothing is shown to players. Phase 9 will use it as social memory.
